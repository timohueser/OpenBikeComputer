Ahead, as a line

What's next for the bike computer: the places on the route that have something to offer, your waypoints, and water and shops between places, on one line coloured by gradient. Drawn at the device's 240 × 320 with its own fonts. Use the keys under the screen or the arrow keys, Enter and Esc.

What riders carry

  • No arrival times. No sheet that riders make for an unsupported race has clock times. Times appear only as outside deadlines, such as control hours.
  • Distances, cumulative and to the next stop. Cue sheets, data books and road books keep a km column.
  • Services as codes per town, in a fixed order. Adventure Cycling uses letter codes, AWOL a right-hand icon column, TfL flag boxes after each name.
  • Gaps above all. "Last store for 46 mi", "no services for 55 mi", and a water alert on the last source when the next is far away.
  • Ahead is down in road books, data books, cue sheets and railway line diagrams. Only scrolls and strip maps put ahead up.

Sheets riders get or make

  • RAAM route book: one spread per time station with map, profile and a turn table; warns where the last 24-hour services are. no ETAs
  • Atlas Mountain Race and Silk Road Mountain Race manuals: resupply tables with total and leg km, and the hardest sections. distance only
  • Tour Divide resupply sheet (Sarah Swallow): one line per place with mile, distance to the next and service codes. distance only
  • Tour Divide cue cards (John Schilling): mile, miles to next, place, food or water; climbs and water get rows; a colour per category. distance only
  • Tour Divide preparation: a printed profile marked with resupply points and checked opening hours. hours only
  • Transcontinental Race planning: handwritten stage sheets with the towns, the bigger towns with services and the climbs. distance only
  • Highland Trail 550: resupply in route order, with the last big shop before the remote section. hours only
  • Colorado Trail Databook: miles both ways, elevation, icons; water reliability as a full, half or empty cup. distance only
  • Arizona Trail water sources: every source near the trail, with dated reports on flow. report dates
  • Adventure Cycling service codes: G, CS, R, CG, M, and NS for a town with no services. distance only
  • RUSA cue sheets: cumulative and leg distance, controls in bold, the last store before a long gap flagged. control hours
  • Audax UK route sheets: A6 folds, distances that reset at each stage, text to read at a glance. distance only
  • Paris–Brest–Paris controls: stage km, cumulative km, climbing and service icons per control. control hours
  • Tour de France stem sticker: the profile with pins for climbs and sprints, each with its km. distance only
  • Unbound Gravel athlete guide: water oases and checkpoints by mile; no stores between checkpoints. cutoffs
  • Trans Iowa cue cards: cumulative mileage, turn and road; towns left off on purpose. distance only
  • Profile stickers: a vertical profile from mile 0 to the finish with aid stations and climbs. optional
  • Baja Divide guide: service codes per town and a chart of distances between them. distance only
  • Hope 1000: fountains every 5 to 10 km and the usual village-shop hours instead of a list. hours only

Other traditions and products

  • AWOL, The A.T. Guide: rows spaced by mileage, icons in one right-hand column, the profile drawn behind the rows. distance only
  • FarOut: a list with distance ahead or behind, and filters for water and camping. distance only
  • Halfmile PCT maps: climb and descent for each 2-mile block, two-letter codes for waypoints. distance only
  • PCT Water Report and Data Book: a water alert when the next water is more than 12 mi away. report dates
  • Te Araroa trail notes: each section as a distance and an effort range. durations
  • Adventure Cycling maps: service symbols per town and named stretches with no services. distance only
  • Ogilby, Britannia (1675): strip maps where side roads are short labelled stubs. distance only
  • Ribbon Map of the Father of Waters (1866): the Mississippi on an 11 ft ribbon, wound past a window with a crank. distance only
  • AAA TripTik: a flipbook of strip maps with short notes on what lies ahead. distance only
  • Railway Sectional Appendix: a vertical line diagram with km on the left and remarks on the right. distance only
  • TfL line diagram standard: ticks for stations, circles for interchanges, fixed-order boxes after each name. no times
  • Rally road book: total km and km since the last box, even boxes, three danger levels. distance only
  • Garmin ClimbPro and Up Ahead: icon, name and distance; the course end stays on screen. some times
  • Hammerhead CLIMBER: 100 m cells by gradient colour, with the next gradients as numbers. no times
  • Wahoo gradient colouring: five colour bands on a transflective screen. time to summit
  • Komoot: a duration from your fitness setting, and a breakdown of surfaces. durations
  • Ride with GPS cue sheets: route cues on the sheet, nearby places only on the map. distance only
  • CyclingCols and Climbfinder: a colour and a number on each segment, counting down to the summit. optional

Data. Places, water, shops and the other services: © OpenStreetMap contributors. The Grimsel heights contain modified Copernicus data; the Kandel loop is a recorded ride with its own heights. Water and shops count when they are within 100 m of the route, other services within 150 m.

Illustrative. The three Grimsel waypoints (Picnic, Viewpoint, Camp) are invented to show your own waypoints. Cafés, toilets, shelters, fuel stations and cash machines are not in the device map today.

Camping rating. Red: inside a nature reserve, wildlife rest zone or national park, where camping is explicitly forbidden and fines are enforced. Otherwise the hand-written rule for the canton decides, falling back to the country: green where camping is allowed (Obwalden allows one night), yellow where it is not allowed but not hard forbidden (Valais). A municipality with its own rule would win over its canton; none of the test spots is in one. Canton rules follow hikebeast.ch's survey of the cantonal laws and need checking before use. Protected areas are OpenStreetMap's, which are complete in Germany and patchy in Switzerland. The Aletsch horizon is cut at the edge of the terrain tile in the west, so its evening sun is shown too late.

Sleep here and Help. Water, roads, buildings and huts come from OpenStreetMap. Slope, hollows and the mountain horizon come from the Copernicus heights; the sun follows the NOAA solar position formulas for 20 September 2026. Swiss grid coordinates use swisstopo's approximate formulas. Back from a screen returns to the Assistant menu.

Rules in this prototype. A place shows when it has at least one of the icons switched on; your waypoints, passes and the route end always show. Between places, only water and shops get a row. A water or shop icon is drawn inverted when the next one is more than 15 km or 500 m of climbing away.
